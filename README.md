## Agile Reflections

## 1. Reflection on the Agile Team Charter

Creating the Agile Team Charter helped me understand the importance of establishing clear roles, responsibilities, goals, and communication methods before starting a group project. It was important for every team member to understand the purpose of VarsityPath and how their contribution supported the project. We identified WhatsApp for quick updates and Microsoft Teams for formal meetings and collaboration. I learned that successful teamwork requires cooperation, accountability, and a shared understanding of project goals. This process showed me that communication and commitment are just as important as technical skills.

## 2. Reflection on User Stories and Tasks

Developing user stories helped me understand software development from the user’s perspective rather than focusing only on technical features. We considered the needs of Grade 11 and Grade 12 learners who require support with academic progress, study planning, university exploration, eligibility checks, and applications. Breaking these needs into user stories and smaller tasks made the requirements easier to understand and organise. I learned that user stories should explain who the user is, what they want to achieve, and why the feature is valuable. This process reinforced the importance of understanding users when designing useful digital solutions.

## 3. Reflection on the Product Backlog

Creating the Product Backlog taught me how to organise project requirements into a prioritised list of features and improvements. VarsityPath included planned features such as academic results management, study planning, university exploration, application tracking, and AI-supported assistance. I learned that not every feature can be developed at once, particularly when working with a fixed deadline. Prioritisation helps a team focus on the features that provide the greatest value. This experience showed me that a Product Backlog supports planning, decision-making, and the management of project requirements.

## 4. Reflection on the Sprint Backlog

Working with Sprint Backlogs helped me understand how a large project can be divided into smaller, manageable stages. The planned VarsityPath sprints covered the project foundation, academic management, university exploration and applications, and testing and presentation. I learned that each sprint needs a clear goal and realistic tasks. Breaking work into stages makes it easier to monitor progress and identify outstanding tasks. This process helped me appreciate the importance of time management, flexibility, and regular progress checks when working towards a deadline.

## 5. Reflection on the Agile Logbook

The Agile Logbook helped me recognise the importance of recording project activities, progress, challenges, and decisions. Keeping project records makes it easier to review progress, identify areas for improvement, and understand how individual contributions support the team. I learned that documentation should be maintained consistently rather than completed only at the end. Overall, this process reinforced the value of accountability, organisation, and transparency in teamwork. It also showed me that documenting a project captures both its outcomes and the learning that takes place during development.
